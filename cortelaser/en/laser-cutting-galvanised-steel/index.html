What galvanised sheet is

It is steel sheet continuously hot-dip coated with zinc on both sides. Zinc protects the steel in two ways: as a barrier and, if scratched, by sacrificing itself before the steel. The most common grade for forming is DX51D+Z (EN 10346): Rm 270–500 MPa and elongation of at least 22 %.

Zinc coating

The number after the Z is the zinc mass in grams per square metre, adding both sides together. This is the typical equivalent in thickness per side:

The cut edge

When the sheet is cut, the edge is left without zinc: it is bare steel. Outdoors, the edge is the first place corrosion starts. Bear this in mind when choosing material and deciding whether to paint the part afterwards.

Zinc fumes

Thermal cutting of galvanised steel produces zinc oxide fumes, which can cause metal fume fever if exposure is not controlled. That is why thermal cutting of zinc-coated sheet needs good fume extraction. For you, it does not change the part design.

Frequently asked questions

Galvanised or electrogalvanised?+

Hot-dip galvanised sheet has more zinc (7 to 42 µm per side) and protects more. Electrogalvanised sheet has 2.5 or 5 µm per side and a smoother, more even surface.

Can galvanised sheet be welded?+

Yes, but the zinc burns off in the weld area and gives off fumes. That area is left unprotected and should be repaired.

What coating do you use?+

Say so when you order if you need a specific coating; otherwise we confirm the coating of the available material in the quote.
