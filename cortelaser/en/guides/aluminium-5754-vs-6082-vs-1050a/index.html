Aluminium 5754, 1050A or 6082: which alloy to cut

5754 or 6082 aluminium? 5754 is the general-purpose sheet: it resists corrosion well, welds and bends. 6082 is stronger but bends less well. 1050A is almost pure aluminium: soft and a good conductor. We cut 5754 and 1050A; for 6082, ask us.

The 1xxx, 5xxx and 6xxx series in two lines

1xxx: pure aluminium

EN AW-1050A (Al 99.5). Very soft, very formable, a good electrical and thermal conductor.

5xxx: aluminium-magnesium

EN AW-5754 (AlMg3). Gains strength through cold working. Good corrosion resistance, including marine.

6xxx: aluminium-magnesium-silicon

EN AW-6082 (AlSi1MgMn). Gains strength through heat treatment (T6 temper). The strongest of the three.

The temper shows how the sheet has been hardened (EN 515): H111 is annealed with slight strain hardening, H22 and H32 are half-hard, H24 is half-hard in 1050A, and T6 is solution heat-treated and aged.

5754: the general-purpose sheet

It contains between 2.6 and 3.6 % magnesium. Wieland gives it the top rating for TIG and MIG welding, atmospheric corrosion resistance and protective anodising, and 1–2 in seawater.

Because of those properties it is the default option for parts that are welded, bent or go into damp environments. In H111 temper it is easier to bend; in H22/H32, stronger.

1050A: soft and conductive

It is aluminium with a minimum of 99.5 %. Its strength is low, but it deforms very well and conducts electricity and heat well: Wieland gives an electrical conductivity of 34–36 MS/m and a thermal conductivity of 210–220 W/(m·K).

Its conductivity and ease of forming suit it to electrical and thermal parts, and to decorative parts that carry no load. In seawater and MIG welding it scores worse than 5754 (Wieland).

6082: structural (ask us)

It is the highest-strength structural alloy in the 6000 series, according to the Righton Blackburns datasheet: it is used in highly stressed parts, structures, cranes and transport equipment. In T6 sheet from 0.4 to 6 mm, EN 485-2 requires Rm ≥ 310 MPa and Rp0.2 ≥ 260 MPa: more than three times the yield strength of 5754 H111.

The trade-off is bending. The Color Metal table requires a minimum radius of 4.5 times the thickness for 6082 T651 from 3 to 6 mm, compared with 1 times for 5754 H111. For protective anodising and MIG welding, Wieland gives it the top rating.

Availability We don't keep 6082 as a stock grade. If your part needs it, ask us when you order and we tell you whether we can source it.

Laser cutting aluminium

Aluminium reflects infrared in the solid state, but IPG Photonics, a manufacturer of fibre laser sources, points out that for practical purposes of fibre cutting it is not considered a reflective metal. It cuts without trouble on today's machines.

The usual gas is nitrogen (Bodor). The Fabricator adds that compressed air often leaves a clean-looking edge on aluminium. Rime warns that with fibre and nitrogen there is almost always some burr that has to be removed.

As a rule of thumb, aluminium needs holes of at least 1.5 times the thickness, like stainless: we detail it in the design guide. We cut up to 6 mm.

Which to choose?

  • 5754: almost always. Parts that are welded, bent or go outdoors or into a marine environment.
  • 1050A: electrical or thermal parts, decorative or heavily formed parts, with no load.
  • 6082: structural parts that are flat or have few bends with a generous radius. Ask us.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between 5754 and 6082 aluminium?+

5754 is a magnesium alloy that bends and welds well and resists marine corrosion. 6082 is heat-treated: much stronger, but it needs considerably larger bend radii.

Do you cut 6082?+

We don't keep it as a stock grade. Ask us when you order and we tell you whether we can source it.

Which aluminium is best for outdoors?+

5754, for its resistance to atmospheric and marine corrosion.

What is H111 temper?+

Annealed with slight strain hardening. It is the softest and easiest-to-bend temper of 5754.
