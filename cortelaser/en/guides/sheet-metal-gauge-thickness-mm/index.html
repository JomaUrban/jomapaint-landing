Sheet metal thickness: standard thicknesses, gauges and mill tolerances

In Europe sheet is ordered in millimetres, but many drawings and catalogues still talk in gauges. Here you have standard sheet thicknesses, a sheet metal gauge to mm chart by material and how much the actual thickness can vary according to the standard.

Which sheet thicknesses are standard?

No standard sets a single list of thicknesses. Each stockholder has its own, and they are very similar. A real example, the list of a German sheet stockholder:

DC01 cold-rolled steel
0.50 · 0.63 · 0.75 · 0.88 · 1.00 · 1.25 · 1.50 · 1.75 · 2.00 · 2.50 · 2.99 · 4.00 mm
Aluminium

Source: Bucher Stahl sheet and aluminium catalogues. If you are designing a new part, use a listed thickness: an unusual thickness may mean buying a whole sheet or waiting for material.

Sheet metal gauge to mm chart

Gauge is an American and British system that dates from the 19th century. The number is not linear and does not mean the same in every material: 16 gauge is 1.52 mm in steel and 1.29 mm in aluminium.

If your drawing says "16 ga", first check which material it is, convert with its column and order in millimetres. If in doubt, put the gauge and material in the notes and we check it with you.

Which standard sets the thickness tolerance?

The actual thickness of a sheet is never exact. Each product has its own dimensional tolerance standard, separate from the material standard:

Thickness tolerances: reference values

The tolerance depends on the thickness, the sheet width and, in steel, its yield strength. This table gives the normal tolerance for sheets in the usual sizes (1250 and 1500 mm wide). The standards also have a tighter "special" tolerance, which has to be specifically requested when buying the sheet.

Assumed widths: 1200–1500 mm (EN 10131, steel with Re < 260 MPa; EN 10143), 1000–1300 mm (EN ISO 9445-2, method A) and 1000–1250 mm (EN 485-4; group I includes 1050A, group II includes 5754). For a laser-cut part this has a practical consequence: the cut follows your outline, but the thickness is that of the sheet. If a fit depends on the actual thickness (a slot, for example), size it with the tolerance in this table. We explain it in tab and slot.

Sheet weight by thickness

The weight per square metre is the thickness in millimetres times the density. For 1 mm thickness:

Thicknesses we cut

We cut sheet up to 6 mm in all five materials: cold-rolled, electrogalvanised, galvanised, stainless and aluminium. We confirm the exact thickness we have in each material in the quote.

Frequently asked questions

How many millimetres is 16 gauge sheet?+

It depends on the material: 1.52 mm in steel, 1.61 mm in galvanised, 1.59 mm in stainless and 1.29 mm in aluminium.

Why does my 2 mm sheet measure 1.92 mm?+

Because it is within tolerance. For 2 mm cold-rolled steel 1250 mm wide, EN 10131 allows ±0.11 mm at normal tolerance.

Which standard governs aluminium sheet thickness?+

EN 485-4 for cold-rolled sheet. The tolerance depends on the thickness, the width and the alloy group.

What is the maximum thickness you cut?+

Up to 6 mm. We confirm the available thickness in each material in the quote.
