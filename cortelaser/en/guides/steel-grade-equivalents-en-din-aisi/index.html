How a steel is named in Europe

Every European steel has two names. The steel name (EN 10027-1) says what it is for or what it contains: DC01 is a cold-rolled sheet for forming, S235JR a structural steel with a minimum yield strength of 235 MPa, X5CrNi18-10 a stainless steel with 18 % chromium and 10 % nickel. The material number (EN 10027-2) is a five-digit code: 1.0330, 1.4301.

The old DIN names (St 12-03, St 37-2, St 02 Z) come from German standards that have been withdrawn, but they are still on many drawings. AISI is the American numbering for stainless steels (304, 316L). For aluminium, Europe uses EN AW with a four-digit number and a chemical designation in square brackets; Germany also used a 3.xxxx material number.

Before using the table An equivalent is not an identity. Two standards can set different composition or property limits for "equivalent" grades. If the part is critical, order the material by its EN designation and check the certificate.

Cold-rolled steels: DC01, DC03, DC04, DC05 and DC06

These are the thin uncoated steel sheets for bending and drawing (EN 10130). The higher the number, the more formable it is. For laser cutting, DC01 is the general-purpose grade.

Galvanised steel: DX51D, DX52D, DX53D and DX54D

Continuously galvanised sheet is ordered to EN 10346. The letter D indicates steel for cold forming and the suffix, the coating: +Z is zinc, and the number after it (Z140, Z275) is the zinc mass in g/m² adding both sides together.

Frequently asked questions

Is DC01 the same as St 12?+

Yes, in practice. DC01 (1.0330) replaces the old St 12-03; order DC01 to EN 10130.

Are AISI 304 and 1.4301 the same steel?+

They are the American and European designations of the same type of stainless, X5CrNi18-10. The composition limits of each standard do not match one hundred per cent.

What is 1.4404?+

It is the stainless steel X2CrNiMo17-12-2, AISI 316L: it contains molybdenum and resists chlorides better than 304.

Is AlMg3 the same as 5754?+

Yes. EN AW-5754 is the numerical designation and AlMg3 the chemical one for the same alloy, with German material number 3.3535.

Are S235JR and St 37-2 equivalent?+

S235JR (1.0038) replaced St 37-2 from DIN 17100, which has been withdrawn.
