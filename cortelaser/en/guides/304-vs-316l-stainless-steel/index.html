304 or 316L stainless for laser-cut parts

304 or 316 stainless steel? The difference is molybdenum: 316L contains it and resists chlorides from the coast, road salt and some chemicals better. 304 is enough indoors and in mild urban environments. We give you manufacturer data to decide.

304, 316L, 1.4301, 1.4404: what they are called

The European standard EN 10088 designates stainless steels with a material number. The names 304 and 316 come from the American designation (AISI/ASTM). They are equivalent:

We cut 1.4301 (304) and 1.4404 (316L). For other equivalents, see the steel equivalents table.

Composition: the molybdenum in 316L

The 2.1 % molybdenum raises the PRE of 316L from 18 to 24. That figure explains almost everything: more resistance to pitting and crevice corrosion in the presence of chlorides.

The low carbon in 316L (the "L") reduces carbide precipitation from welding heat and, with it, the risk of intergranular corrosion next to the weld (Outokumpu).

Corrosion: indoors, city, coast and swimming pools

Outokumpu assigns each grade to a corrosion resistance class (CRC), depending on the environment:

  • Indoor swimming pools. IMOA states that 304 and 316 work well in many pool applications, but must not be used for safety-critical load-bearing elements, such as ceiling hangers: there, steels with 6 % molybdenum are required.
  • Chlorides, stress and heat. Outokumpu warns that 304 and 316L can suffer chloride stress corrosion cracking if stress, chlorides and temperatures above about 50 °C combine.
Indicative choice by environment
EnvironmentGrade Outokumpu proposes
Indoors and mild urban (CRC II–III)304 / 304L
Urban with slight marine influence or pollution (CRC III–IV)316 / 316L
Coast or industrial-marine area (CRC IV–V)Duplex or super-austenitic, not 304 or 316L

Source: Outokumpu. The main driver of atmospheric corrosion of stainless steel is chlorides.

Mechanical properties

Mechanically they are almost the same. The choice is made on corrosion, not strength. Both are austenitic: they bend and weld well with conventional methods, except oxy-acetylene welding, according to Outokumpu.

Sheet finishes: 2B, bright and brushed

EN 10088-2 codes the finishes. The most common on thin sheet:

  • 2B: cold rolled, annealed, pickled and skin-passed. It is the general-purpose finish; according to ASSDA, its roughness is between 0.1 and 1 µm.
  • 2R (BA): bright annealed, almost mirror-like and smoother than 2B.
  • 2J and 2K: brushed and satin, applied afterwards to the mill sheet.

Outdoors, the finish matters as much as the grade. Outokumpu states that smooth, clean surfaces resist better, and advises against deep brushing near the sea. Tell us which finish you need and we confirm whether we have it.

Laser cutting stainless and its edge

Stainless is cut with nitrogen. Bodor explains why: the inert gas does not oxidise the edge, and that oxidation leads to rust. The edge comes out bright metal, without the dark layer of oxygen cutting. More detail in nitrogen or oxygen.

As a rule of thumb, stainless needs slightly larger holes than steel: of the order of 1.5 times the thickness. We explain it in the design guide. If the sheet has protective film, tell us: we confirm how to cut it when we review the file.

Which to choose?

  • 304 (1.4301): indoors and mild urban outdoors. Outokumpu cites household appliances, kitchen equipment, cladding, balustrades and window frames.
  • 316L (1.4404): outdoors near the sea or salted roads, environments with chemicals or chlorine, parts that are welded and go into an aggressive environment.
  • Neither: load-bearing elements in indoor swimming pools or right on the seafront. Consult a corrosion specialist.

316L contains more alloying elements (nickel and molybdenum) and usually costs more. If in doubt, ask us to quote both and decide with the price in front of you. And remember that no grade replaces maintenance: Outokumpu states that in marine and busy urban environments the surface usually needs washing once a year, and that areas not reached by rain stain sooner.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between 304 and 316 stainless steel?+

316 contains 2 % molybdenum, which improves resistance to chloride corrosion. Mechanically they are almost the same.

Is 304 suitable outdoors?+

In rural or mild urban environments, yes. Near the sea or with road salt, 316L is better.

What does the L in 316L mean?+

Low carbon (0.02 % typical). It reduces the risk of corrosion next to welds.

Which gas is stainless cut with?+

Nitrogen, so that the edge comes out oxide-free.
