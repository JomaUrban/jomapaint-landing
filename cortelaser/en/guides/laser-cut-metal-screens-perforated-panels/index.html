Laser-cut screens and perforated sheet: design without distortion

A laser-cut sheet metal screen is a lot of cutting in a little sheet. If the design doesn't allow for it, the panel comes out wavy. These rules help you draw screens, grilles and perforated panels that come out flat.

Why does perforated sheet distort?

Laser is the thermal process that puts in the least heat, but it does put in heat. In a screen, several causes come together:

  • Concentrated heat. Metal expands where it is heated. If the hot area is surrounded by cold material that stops it expanding, the sheet distorts (SSAB).
  • Cut density. Many cuts close together raise the temperature of the area. LaserBoost points this out especially for grid patterns.
  • Internal stresses. Sheet carries stresses from rolling. When material is removed, they are released unevenly (SSAB).
  • Thickness. Thick sheet is stiffer and distorts less; thin sheet has less mass to spread the heat (SSAB; MachineMFG).

Design rules: bridge, pitch and open area

The bridge is the strip of sheet left between two openings. If it is too narrow, it burns or distorts. LaserBoost publishes this table of minimum spacing (in aluminium and copper it asks for it to equal the thickness):

  • Space the openings apart. It is the first measure LaserBoost proposes against heat, and it also makes cutting cheaper.
  • Control the open area. As a practical rule, MachineMFG states that the risk of distortion rises sharply when more than 50 % of the surface is removed.
  • Spread the pattern. An even pattern is better than a heavily cut area next to a solid one.
  • Leave a solid frame around the pattern. It stiffens the panel and is where the fixings go.
  • Avoid long, narrow strips. LaserBoost flags them as the parts most at risk.

Material and thickness

For the same screen, more thickness means less distortion and more cost. If a dense pattern worries you at 1 or 1.5 mm, try one step up in thickness or open up the pattern.

For outdoors, choose a material that holds up: galvanised if it will be painted, stainless or 5754 aluminium if left exposed. The cut edge of galvanised sheet is left without zinc; we explain it in the galvanised edge.

If the material has protective film LaserBoost warns that, with features less than 8 mm apart, the film may peel off or fail to stick. This often happens with filmed stainless or aluminium screens.

Large panels and joints

A screen panel cannot be larger than the sheet it comes from. Before drawing, check the sheet sizes and divide the surface into modules that fit.

  • Make the pattern continue from one module to the next, so the joint doesn't show.
  • Leave the edge of each module solid, with room for overlaps, bolts or trim profiles.
  • Repeat the same module as many times as you can: one file and one quantity are cheaper than many different files.

Fixings and installation

Draw the fixing holes in the solid frame, not inside the pattern. If the panel goes on a subframe, line the holes up with it and leave clearance for expansion on large outdoor panels.

If the panel is bent afterwards to stiffen it (a flange around the perimeter, for example), send the flat pattern: we cut and your supplier bends. If it is going to be painted, we coordinate the painting separately.

What we do when cutting

The cutting order and the layout on the sheet also play a part. SSAB explains that spreading the parts across the sheet helps distribute the heat, and MachineMFG recommends not concentrating cuts in one area. We deal with that when programming the cut: you don't have to draw anything for it.

When we review the file, if we see bridges that are too narrow or a pattern at risk of distorting, we tell you before cutting.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my perforated sheet come out wavy?+

Because of the concentrated heat from many cuts close together and the release of the sheet's internal stresses. Spacing openings apart, leaving a solid frame or going up a thickness reduces it.

What thickness should I use for a screen?+

It depends on the size and density of the pattern. The more open and the larger the panel, the more thickness it needs to come out flat.

What spacing should I leave between openings?+

At least what the thickness requires (for example, 2.1 mm in 3 mm sheet according to LaserBoost) and, for screens, more.

Can you cut screen panels larger than a sheet?+

Not in one piece. They are divided into modules that fit the sheet size and joined on installation.
