Minimum hole, slot and bridge in laser cutting by thickness

The minimum hole diameter in laser cutting depends on thickness and material. There is no universal figure: each fabricator publishes its own. Here are the general rules from several sources, compared, so you can design with margin.

Request a quote All guides

Why is there a minimum hole diameter?

Every hole starts with a pierce: the laser goes through the sheet at one point and then follows the outline. In a small hole, the pierce and the cut concentrate a lot of heat in little material. The result can be a tapered hole, with dross or distorted.

The thicker the sheet, the more heat is needed and the larger the minimum. That is why all the rules are expressed in terms of the thickness (t).

Protolabs, Ponoko, SendCutSend and Fractory give a single rule for all materials. The sources range from 0.3 to 1.5 times the thickness: the most aggressive state what the machine can manage to cut; the most conservative, what comes out well reliably.

Our form uses Fabcon's conservative rule: when you upload a DXF, it warns you if a hole is smaller than 1 × t in cold-rolled, electrogalvanised or galvanised steel, or 1.5 × t in stainless or aluminium. It is a warning, not a rejection: we review it with your file.

Here the sources disagree. Fabcon asks for 1.5 times the thickness in stainless and aluminium. 247TailorSteel publishes values for stainless equal to or smaller than those for nitrogen-cut steel, and for aluminium, slightly larger at some thicknesses.

What to do about that discrepancy: if the hole is functional (a bolt, a shaft), design to the conservative rule. If it is decorative, you can go smaller, and we review it with your file.

Minimum bridge and edge distance

The bridge is the material left between two cuts, or between a hole and the edge. If it is too narrow, it overheats, distorts or burns.

Minimum bridge and distances according to different sources
SourceWhat it says
FabconHole to edge ≥ 1 × t; between holes ≥ 2 × t
ProtolabsHole to edge ≥ 1.58 mm in sheet up to 0.92 mm; ≥ 3.18 mm in thicker sheet

Slots and elongated holes

A narrow slot builds up more heat than a round hole of the same width. Fabcon explains that slots show more taper and heat effect as thickness increases, and that their width is harder to control than a hole's.

What if you need a smaller hole?

  • Draw it as you need it. The form will warn you, but we receive it anyway and review it.
  • Ask about the material. Going down a thickness or changing material can solve it.
  • Drill afterwards. For precision holes or threads, a common option is to leave the hole uncut and drill or machine it afterwards in your workshop.
  • Check the tolerance. A cut hole has the cutting tolerance, not that of a reamed hole. We cover it in laser cutting tolerances.

We cut sheet up to 6 mm. Upload your file and we confirm holes, slots and bridges when we review it.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum hole diameter in laser cutting?+

As a conservative rule, equal to the thickness in steel and 1.5 times the thickness in stainless and aluminium. Some sources go down to half the thickness or less.

Do you publish a table measured on your machines?+

Not for now. We use general industry rules and confirm each case when we review your file.

Can I cut a 2 mm hole in 3 mm sheet?+

According to several industry tables, it is possible in steel; under the 1 × t rule, it falls below. Send it and we review it with your material.

What distance should I leave between a hole and the edge?+

Fabcon recommends at least once the thickness between hole and edge, and twice the thickness between holes.
