Does the edge of laser-cut galvanised sheet rust?

When galvanised sheet is laser cut, the edge is left as bare steel. Does it rust? It depends on the thickness, the zinc and the environment. The zinc on the faces protects the edge over a short distance; beyond that, rust appears. We tell you what the sources say and how to avoid it.

What happens to the zinc when the sheet is cut?

Galvanised sheet (DX51D+Z, EN 10346) arrives with zinc on both faces. When it is cut, the edge is left as uncoated steel: a strip as wide as the sheet is thick. On 1 mm sheet, the exposed edge is 1 mm; on 3 mm sheet, 3 mm.

With a guillotine, some of the zinc is dragged over the edge. With a laser, it isn't: the International Zinc Association (IZA) states that disc, abrasive or laser cutting leaves no zinc on the edge. Protection has to come from the zinc left on the faces.

Cathodic protection: how much edge the zinc protects

Zinc is more active than steel. When there is moisture, a cell forms: the zinc corrodes instead of the steel and its corrosion products seal the bare area (Galvanizers Association). This is sacrificial protection. But it only reaches a short distance, and the sources give different figures:

The AGA and V&S figures refer to scratches and damaged areas, not cut edges; the IZA figures refer to sheet edges. The IZA adds that the protected distance grows with the conductivity and thickness of the moisture film.

Practical reading: on thin sheet, the zinc on the faces covers almost all the edge; from 2–3 mm and outdoors, you are likely to see rust on the edge. The IZA notes that on its 2.3 mm sample the rust was less after 6 months, because the zinc corrosion products ended up covering the steel.

Is edge rust serious?

Structurally, no. The IZA is clear: red rust on the edge may be unacceptable aesthetically, but it does not reduce the structural integrity of the part. The problem is usually visual: a brown line on the edge and, with rain, streaks running onto the galvanised face.

If the part goes indoors, in the dry, the edge normally gives no trouble. If it goes outdoors and is visible, it is worth planning for.

How much zinc the sheet carries: from Z100 to Z600

The EN 10346 designation gives the grams of zinc per square metre, adding both faces together. More zinc means more reserve to protect the edge: the IZA notes that protection improves the higher the ratio of zinc surface to steel surface, and the AGA that coating life is proportional to its thickness.

Electrogalvanised sheet (+ZE, EN 10152) carries much less: between 2.5 and 5 µm per side according to ArcelorMittal, intended for indoor use. On its cut edge, zinc protection is very limited. We compare them in galvanised, electrogalvanised or cold-rolled.

Continuously galvanised sheet versus hot-dip galvanising after cutting

DX51D+Z sheet is galvanised continuously, in coil, before it is cut. Hot-dip galvanising (EN ISO 1461) is done once the part has been made, so the zinc also covers the edges.

Continuous (DX51D+Z)

A coating that is almost all pure zinc, with very little iron-zinc alloy. According to HDGASA, it protects cut edges less, especially on thick sheet with a thin coating.

Hot-dip after fabrication

Thicker coating, with iron-zinc alloy, and coated edges. HDGASA gives local minimums of 35 µm on steel under 1.5 mm and 70 µm on steel over 6 mm.

If you are going to hot-dip galvanise your cut parts, bear in mind a warning from the AGA: edges cut with high-energy processes can harden and make it harder for the zinc to bond, with a risk of flaking. They are inspected visually and repaired to ASTM A780. Discuss it with your galvanizer.

If the part goes outdoors: what to do

  1. Paint. It is the most complete solution: paint covers faces and edges. ArcelorMittal states that galvanised sheet is painted after degreasing and applying a surface treatment; don't paint it unprepared.
  2. Hide the edge. The IZA mentions trim profiles, adhesive tapes or folded hems to cover edges where appearance matters.
  3. Repair with zinc. For touch-ups, the AGA refers to ASTM A780 and the Galvanizers Association to zinc-rich paints or pastes.
  4. Go up a coating or zinc thickness if the environment is aggressive, or hot-dip galvanise after cutting.

If you are going to weld the part, the Galvanizers Association recommends removing the zinc at least 25 mm either side of the weld, ventilating well because of the zinc oxide fumes, and repairing the area afterwards.

Frequently asked questions

Does the edge of laser-cut galvanised sheet rust?+

On thin sheet indoors, normally not. On sheet of 2–3 mm or more outdoors, you are likely to see rust on the edge, although the zinc on the faces slows it down.

How much edge does the zinc protect?+

The sources talk about a few millimetres: from 2 to 7 mm depending on the environment and the test. Beyond that, protection is partial.

Does edge rust weaken the part?+

No. According to the International Zinc Association it is an aesthetic problem, not a structural one.

Does laser cutting burn off the zinc?+

The cut leaves the edge without zinc, just as a disc or abrasive does. Protection comes from the zinc on the faces.
