What electrogalvanised sheet is

It is cold-rolled steel sheet onto which zinc is deposited by electrolysis: the strip passes through a bath of zinc salts and the zinc adheres to its surface. The layer is much thinner than hot-dip galvanising, even, and with good surface quality.

The standard is EN 10152. The base steel is one of the cold-rolled grades, designated by adding +ZE: for example, DC01+ZE. ArcelorMittal makes it from 0.40 to 3 mm thick.

Zinc coating

The two numbers in the designation are the nominal zinc thickness on each side, in tenths of a micron: ZE25/25 has 2.5 µm per side. There is also sheet with zinc on one side only.

The cut edge

When the sheet is cut, the edge is left without zinc: it is bare steel. With such a thin zinc layer, bear this in mind if the part will be in a damp environment.

Zinc fumes

Thermal cutting of zinc-coated sheet produces zinc oxide fumes, which can cause metal fume fever if exposure is not controlled. That is why thermal cutting of zinc-coated sheet needs good fume extraction. For you, it does not change the part design.

Frequently asked questions

Electrogalvanised or galvanised?+

Electrogalvanised sheet has much less zinc (2.5 or 5 µm per side) and a smooth, even surface. Hot-dip galvanised sheet has between 7 and 42 µm per side and protects more. If the part goes outdoors, galvanised protects more; if in doubt, tell us the use.

Can it be painted?+

Yes. ArcelorMittal states that, phosphated or with a thin organic coating, it can be painted directly. Parts leave here unpainted.

Can it be welded?+

Yes. SSAB highlights its good resistance spot weldability. The zinc is lost in the welded area.
